If your AI images look a little flat, a little stock, a little "AI", the problem usually isn't the tool. It's the order you write the prompt in.

Image models read a prompt from the start. What comes first gets the most weight. So when a prompt opens with "beautiful, cinematic, moody, aesthetic", the model spends its attention on vibes before it even knows what it's drawing.

I write every image prompt in the same order, whether I'm in ChatGPT, Midjourney, Nano Banana or Canva's AI tools. I call it the Sought After Studio Prompt Method. Here it is, step by step.

## The build order

Write the blocks in this order. Skip one if it doesn't apply, but never shuffle them.

1. **Subject.** One plain clause. No adjectives yet. *A ceramic mug on a wooden table.*
2. **Spatial relationships.** If there's more than one thing in the frame, say exactly where each one sits. *The mug in the foreground right, a linen napkin folded beside it, a small vase behind them on the left.*
3. **Reference role.** If you upload a reference image, say what it's for: the shape, the colour, the material or the mood. Not all four.
4. **Material and texture.** What things are made of and how the surface reacts to light. *Matte stoneware with a speckled glaze.*
5. **Colour.** Name the tone, don't describe a feeling. *Warm oat with a dusty terracotta rim*, not "neutral".
6. **Setting.** One short phrase. *A quiet kitchen counter.*
7. **Lighting.** Direction, quality and source in one line. *Soft window light from camera left, gentle falloff, no fill.*
8. **Camera and lens.** Angle, lens and depth of field. *Shot at eye level on a 50mm lens, shallow depth of field, natural film grain.*
9. **Style and mood.** Three to five words, at the very end. *Calm, editorial, Scandinavian.*
10. **Negative control.** Only for the thing the tool keeps getting wrong. *No text on the mug.*

Notice where the mood words went: last. By the time the model reads them, everything that matters is already decided.

## The full prompt

Put together, the mug example reads like this:

```Copy prompt
A ceramic mug on a wooden table. The mug in the foreground right, a linen napkin folded beside it, a small vase with one dried stem behind them on the left. Matte stoneware with a speckled glaze, the napkin in soft washed linen. Warm oat mug with a dusty terracotta rim, natural oak table. A quiet kitchen counter. Soft window light from camera left, gentle falloff, no fill. Shot at eye level on a 50mm lens, shallow depth of field, natural film grain. Calm, editorial, Scandinavian. No text or logos on the mug.
```

Swap the subject for your own product and keep the rest. You'll see the difference straight away.

## Three rules that make it work

### Name exact values, not feelings

"Warmer", "better", "more natural": a model can't act on a feeling. Name the tone, the texture or the angle instead.

- Instead of *good lighting*, write *soft light from camera left, gentle shadow underneath*.
- Instead of *nice colour*, write *warm terracotta, slightly dusty, not saturated*.
- Instead of *make it pop*, write *higher contrast, one saturated accent colour only*.
- Instead of *a few things on the table*, write *a mug left of centre, a notebook angled beside it, a pen resting on top*.

### Lock only what matters

Pick the two or three things that really can't change: maybe the product shape and the brand colour. Leave everything else open. When you lock everything, the result gets stiff and generic.

### Fix one thing at a time

Got an image that's almost right? Don't rewrite the whole prompt. Say the one change, then lock the rest:

```Copy the fix prompt
Change only the mug colour to deep forest green with a matte finish. Keep the camera angle, framing, composition, lighting direction and every other object exactly the same.
```
